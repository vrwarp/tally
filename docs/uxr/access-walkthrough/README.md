# Team and access — a walkthrough

Every screen here is the live app, signed in against the seeded emulator and photographed as the person it was designed for would meet it. The design it implements is docs/team-access.md, which converged over five rounds of critique and one review by the owner.

> Generated by `npm run walkthrough:access`. Do not edit by hand.

## The fence

### Tonight’s gatherings, and the ones that are not yours

Locked, not hidden. A counselor at a door at 6:59 who sees an empty screen concludes the app is broken; one who sees a lock and a name knows what to do.

![Tonight’s gatherings, and the ones that are not yours](shots/desktop-08-tonight-s-gatherings-and-the-ones-that-are-not-yours.png)

![Tonight’s gatherings, and the ones that are not yours, on a phone](shots/phone-09-tonight-s-gatherings-and-the-ones-that-are-not-yours.png)

### The roster, and who is on it

The chip says how many people can work this gathering. The dot beside it says somebody is asking to be added — eight pixels, no word, no target of its own, because a strip above the first row would push every name under a descending thumb.

![The roster, and who is on it](shots/desktop-09-the-roster-and-who-is-on-it.png)

![The roster, and who is on it, on a phone](shots/phone-10-the-roster-and-who-is-on-it.png)

### Who’s on, and who is asking

The sheet leads with the ask, because it is the only thing here that is somebody’s to do. Add puts them on; Clear says it was answered — and marks it, so the person who asked can tell that from nobody having looked.

![Who’s on, and who is asking](shots/desktop-10-who-s-on-and-who-is-asking.png)

![Who’s on, and who is asking, on a phone](shots/phone-11-who-s-on-and-who-is-asking.png)

### A gathering you are not on

Full names, whoever opened Tally today first, and an admin unconditionally — the person the list names may have been on leave since June. Under them, one button, which puts your name on the Add list and never claims to be a queue.
